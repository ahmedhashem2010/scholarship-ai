# SmartScholar — brand identity

**This file is the source of truth.** The design drifted three times because
decisions lived in people's heads and in scattered CSS. If a colour, font or
spacing value isn't in here, it doesn't exist. Change it here first, then in
code.

Everything below is derived from the logo. The palette is the logo's two
colours plus what they need around them; the type is chosen to sit with them;
the tone follows from both. Nothing here was picked because it looked nice on
its own.

---

## 1 · The mark

An **S drawn as a road**. Lane dashes down the middle, a start node
bottom-left, a waypoint in the belly, a gold destination top-right.

It works because it isn't a metaphor bolted onto a letter — the letter *is* the
route. That's the product in one shape: a deadline becomes a path with steps on
it, and the last step is arrival rather than another search result.

It also passes the tests a graduation cap fails:

| Test | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Legible at 16px | The browser tab is where people mostly see it |
| Works in one colour | Print, embroidery, WhatsApp avatars |
| Reversed on dark | Half the app is dark mode |
| No gradient dependency | Gradients die in email clients and favicons |
| Animatable | The road can draw itself — see the launch film |

### Files — `public/brand/`

All generated by `node scripts/build-brand.mjs` from `logo-source.svg`.
**Never hand-edit the outputs.** Change the source, re-run the script.

| File | Use |
|---|---|
| `logo-source.svg` | The master artwork. The only file you edit |
| `logo-mark.svg` | **The one components import.** Cropped tight, full colour |
| `favicon.svg` | 16–32px. Lane dashes removed, nodes enlarged |
| `app-icon.svg` | Rounded tile, navy on white |
| `app-icon-dark.svg` | Rounded tile, white on navy, gold node kept |
| `logo-mark-mono.svg` | One ink, holes knocked out. Print, embroidery |
| `logo-lockup.svg` | Mark + wordmark, for slides and exports only |

**Two viewBoxes, deliberately.** The artwork has heavy padding — the road
occupies under half the 1024 canvas. That's right for an app icon, where the OS
expects margin inside the tile, and wrong everywhere else: at 16px it leaves
about seven real pixels of mark. So icons keep the full canvas and everything
else crops to `185 172 660 660`.

**The favicon drops the lane dashes.** At 16px each dash is under a pixel wide
and they grey the road into mud. The road, the nodes and the gold node survive;
the texture doesn't. That's a detail budget, not a different logo.

> ⚠️ `logo-mark-mono.svg` uses `fill-rule: evenodd` to cut its holes. Browsers
> support this; the renderer used to preview it here did not, so that one file
> is **unverified**. Open it in a browser before sending it to a printer.

### Rules

- **Clear space** on all sides = the diameter of the gold node.
- **Never** recolour the road, drop the gold node, add a gradient, rotate the
  mark, or set it inside any shape other than the app-icon tile.
- **Minimum size 16px.** Below that, use nothing.
- On navy or any dark surface: white road, gold node kept. Gold is the one
  colour that never changes.
- In the app, compose the mark and live text. Don't use `logo-lockup.svg` — its
  wordmark is SVG text and falls back to a system font on machines without Plex.

## 2 · Colour

Two colours come from the logo. Everything else exists to support them.

**Navy is the voice. Gold is the reward.** Navy carries structure, text,
buttons, the mark. Gold appears when a student *arrives* at something —
verified, eligible, completed, matched. If gold is everywhere it stops meaning
arrival, which is exactly how the previous teal system died.

### Navy — `#162C4C`

```
--ink-900   #0D1A2D    dark-mode page ground
--ink-800   #162C4C    THE LOGO. Headings, primary buttons
--ink-700   #1F3D63    button hover
--ink-600   #2B527F    dark-mode surfaces
--ink-500   #3B6A9E    links, interactive text on light   (5.2:1 on white)
--ink-300   #93A8C4    dark-mode secondary text
--ink-200   #C6D2E1    dividers on tinted surfaces
--ink-100   #E5EBF3    selected rows, subtle fills
--ink-50    #F2F6FA    table stripes, hover rows
```

### Gold — `#C6A14B`

```
--gold-700  #8A6E2C    gold TEXT
--gold-500  #C6A14B    THE LOGO. Badges, the destination, accents
--gold-200  #EDE0BE    badge backgrounds
--gold-50   #FBF7EC    highlight blocks
```

**Never set body text in `--gold-500`.** It's 2.1:1 on white — effectively
invisible to anyone with less than perfect sight, and a fail under WCAG at any
size. Use `--gold-700` on light, `--gold-200` on dark.

### Calm neutrals

Warm, not grey. Cold greys fight gold and make navy look like a corporate
default. A paper-white ground makes both look chosen.

```
--paper     #FAF9F6    page background
--card      #FFFFFF    cards, raised surfaces
--line      #E8E4DC    borders, dividers
--muted     #5F6B7F    secondary text          (5.9:1 on paper)
--dim       #8C97A8    placeholders, metadata  (3.4:1 — never body text)
```

### Meaning colours — four, each with exactly one job

Muted deliberately. Bright semantic colours on a navy-and-gold ground look like
error dialogs, and this product should feel calm about a stressful thing.

```
--eligible  #2E7D62    you qualify · verified · step done
--urgent    #BF6B3A    deadline close · action needed soon
--blocked   #A8443A    you don't qualify · expired · something broke
--unknown   #6B7387    the source didn't say
```

> **The most important colour rule: `--unknown` is its own colour.**
>
> Your entire trust proposition is admitting what the source didn't state. Most
> platforms fold that into a warning colour, or worse into the success colour.
> A distinct, deliberately unexciting slate — paired with a **dashed** border,
> never solid — says *we don't know* without saying *be alarmed*.
>
> It is not amber, because amber sits next to gold and gold means arrival. Two
> meanings competing for one hue is how a palette stops communicating.

### Dark mode

```
--bg #0D1A2D   --card-dark #14263F   --line-dark #22374F   --text-dark #E8EEF6
```

Gold stays `#C6A14B` in both modes. It's the constant.

## 3 · Type

**IBM Plex Sans Arabic + IBM Plex Sans.**

One superfamily, drawn together, metrically harmonised. This solves what broke
the last three attempts: an Arabic face and a Latin face chosen separately never
sit on the same line properly, and every fix creates a new mismatch elsewhere.
Here they're the same design.

It suits the mark, too. Plex is engineered rather than friendly — slightly
technical, quietly serious, calm at long reading lengths. A geometric face like
Poppins fights navy-and-gold; it reads consumer-app when the brand needs to read
*dependable*.

Both free, on Google Fonts, full weight ranges.

```
Latin:   IBM Plex Sans         400 · 500 · 600 · 700
Arabic:  IBM Plex Sans Arabic  400 · 500 · 600 · 700
```

**Majara and Nitro are dead.** Display faces — no proper Latin glyphs, no
presentation forms for shaping. They looked good on one hero and broke
everywhere else, and they're why the video's Arabic came out unjoined.

### Scale

Only these. No intermediate sizes.

| Token | px (desktop / mobile) | Weight | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| `display` | 56 / 38 | 600 | Landing hero only |
| `h1` | 36 / 28 | 600 | Page title |
| `h2` | 28 / 23 | 600 | Section |
| `h3` | 20 / 18 | 600 | Card title |
| `body` | 16 | 400 | Everything |
| `small` | 14 | 400 | Secondary |
| `caption` | 12 | 500 | Labels, metadata |

Headings at **600, not 700**. Plex at 700 is heavy enough to feel loud, and loud
is the opposite of what this brand is doing.

**16px minimum on form inputs. Non-negotiable.** iOS Safari zooms the page on
any focused control under 16px and never zooms back. That bug made signup feel
broken on iPhone for a week.

### Arabic typography

- Line height **1.8** for Arabic body vs 1.6 Latin. Arabic needs the room;
  Tailwind's default crops descenders.
- **Never** `text-transform: uppercase` on Arabic. It does nothing and signals
  that nobody checked.
- Numbers, emails, URLs and language codes stay LTR inside RTL text:
  `dir="ltr"` with `unicode-bidi: isolate`.
- Don't letter-space Arabic. It breaks the joins.

## 4 · Layout

**Spacing: multiples of 4 only.** `4 8 12 16 24 32 48 64 96`. Nothing else.
Arbitrary values like `p-[13px]` are how a system rots.

**Radius**

| Token | px | Use |
|---|---|---|
| `sm` | 8 | Inputs, chips |
| `md` | 12 | Buttons, badges |
| `lg` | 16 | Cards |
| `xl` | 24 | Modals, hero panels |
| `full` | 999 | Pills, avatars |

The app-icon tile uses a 22.5% corner radius, matching the artwork. That ratio
belongs to the icon — don't apply it anywhere else.

**Logical properties only.** `ms/me`, `ps/pe`, `start/end`, `text-start`. Never
`ml/mr/pl/pr/left/right`. The app is RTL-first and physical directions don't
mirror — that's what put the input icons on top of the text in Arabic.

**Breakpoints:** `xs 400 · sm 640 · md 768 · lg 1024 · xl 1280`.
`xs` exists because Tailwind's smallest default is 640px, which is a tablet.
Without it, 360–390px phones always got the most cramped layout.

**Elevation** — shadows are navy-tinted, never pure black. Black shadows on a
warm paper ground look dirty.

| Level | Shadow | Use |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | none | Page surfaces |
| 1 | `0 1px 2px rgba(22,44,76,.08)` | Cards at rest |
| 2 | `0 6px 20px rgba(22,44,76,.10)` | Hover, dropdowns |
| 3 | `0 20px 44px rgba(22,44,76,.16)` | Modals |

## 5 · Motion

- **Fast** 150ms — hover, focus, colour
- **Base** 250ms — cards, panels, most things
- **Slow** 400ms — page and modal transitions

Easing `cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1)` for entrances, `ease-out` for exits.

**Things arrive; they don't appear.** Position or scale plus a slight overshoot.
A pure opacity fade is the clearest tell of a cheap interface.

**The signature motion is the road drawing itself.** Bottom-left to gold
destination, about 1.8s, eased at both ends — implemented in
`video-assets/logo.py`. Use it for the logo reveal and for anything
representing a completed journey. Don't use it for loading: it means *progress
toward a goal*, not *please wait*.

Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` — cut every duration to 0.01ms.

## 6 · Voice

**Arabic first.** English is the translation, not the other way round.

- Plain and direct, no marketing register. "منحة بدون آيلتس" not
  "فرص تعليمية متميزة".
- **Say what you don't know.** "غير مذكور في المصدر" is the most important
  string in the product.
- Numbers over adjectives. "خلال ١٩ يوماً" beats "قريباً".
- Never promise an outcome. You help people *apply well*; you don't get them
  accepted.
- Calm about stressful things. The student is already anxious. The interface
  shouldn't be.

## 7 · When something isn't specified here

Pick the option that is **more boring and more consistent** with what's already
here, then add it to this file. A system stays coherent by refusing novelty,
not by adding it.
